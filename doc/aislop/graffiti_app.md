## Why the software category still feels missing

I think the distinction you're noticing is important. These tools are mostly text generators or small browser-based editors, rather than full-fledged, general-purpose graffiti typography studios.

There are three different levels of capability:

| Approach | What it gives you | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Graffiti fonts | Type text and apply color | Letter shapes are mostly predetermined |
| Layered text-effect generators | Control color, outline, shadow, and depth | Limited control over letter construction |
| Editable graffiti lettering studio | Edit glyph shapes, positioning, overlaps, colors, and effects independently | More complex to build; advanced tools are less common |

The ideal application would combine the speed of a font editor with the flexibility of a vector drawing program. It would preserve the exact input characters while letting users reshape their appearance.

One particularly important feature would be deterministic text rendering: typing a word should always produce the correct letters. Artistic effects should be adjustable parameters, not unpredictable AI decisions.
