# 🎧 Best Spotify podcasts for learning Spanish as a complete beginner

First, I think your idea is wonderful\! Learning Spanish as a new hobby while walking in a park or travelling by train sounds like a relaxing way to spend your time. You don't need to aim for fluency, take exams, or spend hours studying grammar. You can simply enjoy discovering a few new Spanish words every day. 😊

Since **Thai is your native language and English is your second language**, I recommend starting with Spanish podcasts that explain new words in English, speak clearly, and introduce vocabulary gradually.

However, there's one important detail about your request: **Spotify podcasts don't necessarily have both English and Spanish subtitles.** Many are audio-only, and some provide transcripts or subtitles on a separate website. I'll distinguish between the podcasts themselves and the resources available to help you read along.

## My top 4 recommendations

### 1\. Coffee Break Spanish — Best overall for absolute beginners

**My first choice for you.**

- **Level:** Complete beginner to advanced.
- **Teaching language:** English and Spanish.
- **Episode length:** Approximately 15–20 minutes.
- **Cost:** Free audio lessons; additional learning materials are available for purchase.
- **Best for:** Learning everyday vocabulary, useful expressions, and basic Spanish conversations.

🎧 Listen to Coffee Break Spanish on Spotify

Why I recommend it:

The lessons feature an English-speaking teacher guiding a learner through Spanish, with explanations of vocabulary and grammar along the way. You can learn greetings, introduce yourself, order food, and ask for directions without needing any previous Spanish knowledge.

The first season is designed specifically for beginners. You can follow the lessons in order rather than randomly choosing episodes and struggling with vocabulary that hasn't been introduced yet.

The official website provides information about the free podcast and the additional resources available through its paid courses. The latter include video lessons and written learning materials. However, the free Spotify audio feed does not automatically give you bilingual subtitles. 【turn0search0】

**My suggestion:** Start with Season 1 and listen to one lesson during a walk. Don't worry about understanding every word. Focus on remembering a few expressions you particularly like.

### 2\. ¡Cuéntame\! — Best for relaxed listening and remembering words

- **Level:** Beginner to lower intermediate.
- **Teaching language:** Primarily Spanish, delivered in a learner-friendly way.
- **Best for:** Getting used to Spanish pronunciation and learning vocabulary through stories.

🎧 Find ¡Cuéntame\! on Spotify

This is a good alternative once you want to hear more Spanish and less English.

The podcast uses stories and relatively clear, slower speech to help learners understand Spanish in context. Rather than memorising a list of unrelated words, you hear vocabulary used in situations that help you understand its meaning.

For example, you might hear words related to food, family, daily activities, or travel several times in a story.

A useful feature is that transcript links are available for its episodes, according to a recent comparison of Spanish-learning podcasts. 【turn0search1】

**One limitation:** I wouldn't assume that every transcript includes both English and Spanish. Check the materials accompanying an episode before choosing it specifically for bilingual reading.

**My suggestion:** Try this when you want your listening session to feel more like enjoying a story than attending a lesson.

### 3\. SpanishPod101 — Best for learning practical vocabulary

- **Level:** Complete beginner to advanced.
- **Teaching language:** Many beginner lessons use English explanations alongside Spanish examples.
- **Best for:** Everyday words, greetings, travel expressions, and short conversations.

🎧 Find SpanishPod101 on Spotify

SpanishPod101 offers a large collection of lessons covering practical situations.

You can look for topics such as:

- Saying hello and goodbye.
- Introducing yourself.
- Ordering food and drinks.
- Asking for directions.
- Numbers, dates, and time.
- Common expressions used in everyday conversations.

This makes it particularly suitable for your goal of casually memorising Spanish words rather than following an intensive course.

SpanishPod101 also offers supplementary learning materials, although access to transcripts and other resources can depend on the lesson and membership level. 【turn0search4】

**My suggestion:** Choose lessons about things you encounter in daily life. If you hear Spanish vocabulary related to coffee, food, parks, or train stations, try to remember those words the next time you encounter the same things in real life.

That little connection between a word and your surroundings can make learning more enjoyable.

### 4\. Language Transfer — Best if you enjoy understanding how words work

- **Level:** Complete beginner.
- **Format:** Guided audio lessons.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Best for:** Building a foundation in Spanish and understanding how to form basic sentences.

🎧 Find Language Transfer: Complete Spanish on Spotify

Language Transfer is slightly different from the other recommendations.

Instead of simply presenting vocabulary for you to memorise, the instructor guides a learner through the process of constructing Spanish sentences and understanding how the language works.

This can help you recognise connections between English and Spanish words and understand some of the patterns that make Spanish easier to learn.

However, **it is not my first choice if your main priority is reading English and Spanish subtitles together**. It's an audio-based course, so you'll need a separate written resource if you want to follow along visually.

I recommend it as an optional companion to Coffee Break Spanish rather than something you must complete.

## How to get both English and Spanish subtitles

This is the most important part of your question, so let's address it directly.

Spotify now supports a feature called **Read Along**, which displays episode transcripts for podcasts that offer them. Some shows also have translated transcripts. However, transcript availability depends on the individual show and episode, and you shouldn't assume that every podcast has both English and Spanish text.  Spotify+1

Here's what I recommend:

1. Open a podcast episode in Spotify.
2. Start playing the episode.
3. Open the Now Playing screen and look for the transcript or Read Along section.
4. Check whether the transcript is available and whether translated text is offered.
5. If it isn't available, visit the podcast's official website to look for transcripts or lesson notes.

One small consideration: Spotify's transcripts are text, rather than necessarily being video subtitles displayed over a picture. If you specifically want two languages visible simultaneously, you may need a separate learning resource.

For Coffee Break Spanish, for example, the official website offers additional learning materials through its paid courses, but its free podcast feed is audio-focused.  Coffee Break Languages

### My recommendation for your particular situation

Because you want to listen while walking or travelling, I would prioritise these three things:

- Clear Spanish pronunciation.
- English explanations to help you understand unfamiliar words.
- Short episodes that you can replay without feeling that you're studying for an exam.

You don't necessarily need subtitles visible all the time. In fact, you can get a lot of enjoyment from listening first and checking the written words afterwards.

However, if reading along in both languages is essential, check the transcript arrangements before committing to a podcast.

## A simple Spanish-learning routine for your walks and train journeys

You don't need to set aside additional study time. Try this relaxed routine instead.

| When | What to do | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before your walk | Choose one beginner episode. | 1 minute |
| During your walk | Listen and enjoy the lesson. Focus on words you recognise. | 10–15 minutes |
| During your train journey | Replay the same episode and check its transcript if available. | 5–10 minutes |
| At the end | Pick just three Spanish words to remember. | 1 minute |

For example, you might learn these three words during your first few days:

| Spanish | English | Pronunciation guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hola | Hello | OH-lah |
| Gracias | Thank you | GRAH-syahs |
| Agua | Water | AH-gwah |

The pronunciation guides are approximate, but they're enough to get you started.

You don't need to memorise 20 words every day. **Three words that you remember tomorrow are more useful than 20 words that you forget by the evening.**

### One final tip: Don't worry about your language-learning background

You've mentioned that you've only ever learned Thai and English. That's absolutely fine.

You don't need previous experience learning a third language to start Spanish. Since you're already comfortable using English, you can take advantage of the English explanations in beginner lessons without having to learn through Thai-language teaching materials.

You may also recognise some English words that have connections to Spanish, although not every similar-looking word has the same meaning.

Most importantly, keep this enjoyable. If you miss a day, simply resume when you feel like it. You're building a hobby, not meeting a deadline.
